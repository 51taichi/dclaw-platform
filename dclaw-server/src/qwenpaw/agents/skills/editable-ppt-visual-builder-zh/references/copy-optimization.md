# Copy Optimization Reference

Use this reference before building the story spine, writing slide titles, or generating the final deck. The goal is to turn source material into presentation-grade copy while preserving source truth.

## Principle

A deck is weak when it merely redistributes document paragraphs onto slides. Optimize the copy first, then design around the optimized claims.

## Preserve

- exact company, product, person, customer, role, and project names
- dates, metrics, prices, timelines, responsibilities, and legal/compliance language
- source-defined scope, constraints, and exclusions
- quoted language that the user explicitly wants kept
- uncertainty and missing proof

Do not invent proof, customer names, screenshots, metrics, ROI, capabilities, pricing, guarantees, implementation timelines, or certification claims.

## Extract Commercial Highlights

For every source, extract:

- pain: what is costly, slow, fragmented, risky, or strategically urgent
- stakes: why the audience should act now
- promise: what changes after the solution
- differentiation: why this approach is not a generic service or tool
- proof: metrics, examples, roles, cases, screenshots, implementation artifacts, or acceptance criteria
- operating logic: how the work is delivered, governed, and sustained
- ROI or personnel impact: cost, time, throughput, adoption, risk, or capability leverage
- risk control: what prevents failure
- final ask: what decision or next step is needed

## Rewrite Into Presentation Copy

Convert raw material into:

- claim-led slide headlines
- one-sentence slide takeaways
- section titles that signal story turns
- proof labels and metric captions
- short callouts
- comparison labels
- roadmap and next-step language

Strong headlines make a claim:

- weak: `AI training service`
- stronger: `The training is not the product; sustained adoption is`
- weak: `Three service modules`
- stronger: `Three services form one operating system for enterprise AI adoption`

## Copy Strategy Output

Before the story spine, write:

```text
Copy strategy:
- Core message:
- Audience belief shift:
- Headline style:
- Proof style:
- Commercial highlights:
- Must preserve wording:
- Missing or unsupported proof:
- Forbidden claims:
- Anchor-page candidates:
```

## Slide-Level Copy Direction

For each slide, define:

- source basis
- optimized headline
- takeaway
- commercial highlight
- proof gap
- forbidden claims

## Failure Patterns

- raw source paragraphs become bullets without compression
- slide titles name a topic but do not make a claim
- the deck says "we provide" but not "the buyer gains"
- every benefit sounds generic
- the final ask is missing or vague
- proof gaps are hidden by confident language
- clever wording introduces facts not present in the source
